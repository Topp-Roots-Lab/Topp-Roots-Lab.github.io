## Education

- Ph.D. in Biological Sciences (Plant Molecular Biology and Biochemistry), University of Buenos Aires, Argentina
- B.Sc. in Biochemistry and Molecular Biology (Immunology), University of Havana, Cuba

## Research Interests

Javier’s research focuses on understanding and engineering the molecular and genetic mechanisms that control root development, root system architecture, and plant responses to environmental conditions. His work integrates functional genomics, gene editing, synthetic biology, and quantitative phenotyping in the following areas:

- Identifying genes and regulatory pathways controlling root growth, development, and stress adaptation.
- Applying CRISPR-based genome editing and molecular cloning to validate candidate genes and engineer root traits.
- Developing transient-expression and reporter systems for quantitative characterization of promoters and regulatory elements.
- Combining GWAS, transcriptomics, microscopy, and 3D root phenotyping to connect genetic variation with plant phenotypes.

Javier has applied these approaches in maize, soybean, sorghum, tobacco, and *Arabidopsis thaliana*. His research aims to support the development of crops with improved resource-use efficiency, environmental resilience, and beneficial plant–microbe interactions.
